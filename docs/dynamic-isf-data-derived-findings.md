# Dynamic ISF: can sensitivity be derived from closed-loop data?

**2026-06-07; audited 2026-09-16** · Tim Street / Claude

> This is the corrected evidence summary for the historical feasibility work. The original
> physiological constants and dosing recommendations are withdrawn. See
> `DYNAMIC-ISF-AUDIT-2026-09.md` for the full review.

---

## Question

Can a person's physiological insulin sensitivity factor be calculated from routine closed-loop
glucose, IOB and delivery records well enough to replace an entered ISF?

## Short answer

Not with the estimators tested here.

The analyses can calculate useful outcome proxies. They can compare equations, describe entered
settings and test prediction error inside a loop model. They do not isolate the glucose effect of
absorbed insulin from new insulin delivery, controller response, basal need, endogenous glucose
and residual carbohydrate action. The resulting coefficient should not be called measured ISF.

---

## What the historical programme measured

The work used 138 people from open-source AID cohorts and a series of related analyses. The table
below describes the original outputs using their corrected interpretation.

| analysis | historical result | audited interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| short-window stability | 14-day estimates varied markedly between blocks | the proxy is too unstable for direct dosing calibration |
| variance decomposition | most short-term variation appeared residual | this may be estimator noise and controller feedback, not biological sensitivity variation |
| shrinkage | longer windows and population shrinkage improved repeatability | repeatability of a proxy does not establish physiological validity |
| outcome association | the proxy was associated with existing low-glucose outcomes | this is evidence of endogeneity and makes causal dosing use unsafe |
| overnight restriction | the same bias remained overnight | removing obvious meals did not identify insulin effect |
| outcome-based adjustment | bounded outcome tuning reduced the recorded hypo signature | this is a separate control strategy, not validation of the ISF proxy |

The original work described a square-root TDD relationship and constants near 355 for entered
profiles and 145 for the change-in-IOB proxy. Those numbers remain reproducible descriptions of
their targets. They are not physiological constants and should not be used as dosing defaults.

---

## Why the estimator does not identify ISF

The historical regression related glucose change to change in modelled IOB during a fasting
window. That seems intuitive, but three quantities are mixed together:

1. insulin already active at the beginning of the window;
2. new bolus and temporary-basal insulin delivered during the window;
3. insulin leaving the modelled IOB curve as time passes.

The controller changes the second quantity in response to glucose and trend. The exposure is
therefore selected by the same process that is trying to correct the outcome. A coefficient from
that regression cannot, by itself, be interpreted as glucose lowering per unit of absorbed
insulin.

The later action-balance estimator does not remove the problem. Its IOB is generated by an assumed
insulin-action model, and its delivery term includes suggested SMB and temporary basal rather than
confirmed enacted delivery in every record. It is a second observational proxy, not an independent
ground truth.

## What remains supported

### The equation implementations are auditable

The Python implementation reproduces the intended v1 and author-confirmed v2 equations. V2 uses
`ln(BG/divisor)` without `+1` and floors glucose at `divisor+1`. For the standard rapid-acting
configuration, including NovoRapid, that is divisor/floor 75/76.

### Static ISF is competitive in the tested prediction model

In selected overnight windows, a person's tuned static ISF predicted the realised end glucose
about as well as the loop's own calibrated ISF and better than either dynamic equation. This is a
within-loop prediction result. It does not measure the biological ISF level.

### The tested data do not support a glucose multiplier

With contiguous time-fold validation, the best fitted glucose exponent remained zero and the
glucose term improved median absolute error by 0.0 mg/dL. Within these data and this prediction
model, adding glucose-dependent sensitivity did not help.

### Residual carbohydrate is a credible explanation

Separate post-COB analyses found lower apparent insulin effect soon after recorded COB cleared.
This is consistent with carbohydrate absorption continuing after the controller's COB model has
ended. It supports the hypothesis that some apparent Dynamic ISF behaviour compensates for an
imperfect carbohydrate model. It does not prove that every successful use of Dynamic ISF has that
cause.

---

## What is withdrawn

The audit withdraws these earlier claims:

- that physiological ISF follows `1/√TDD`;
- that constants derived from the change-in-IOB proxy are measured ISF constants;
- that a per-person physiological ISF can be safely calibrated from these closed-loop records;
- that the Diabeloop-shaped glucose multiplier is supported by this cohort;
- that the historical Tier 1 or Tier 2 v-next forms are ready for live dosing.

The entered profile ISF remains a description of how people configured their systems. It should
not be presented as a biological measurement either.

---

## Practical conclusion

Routine closed-loop records are valuable for equation replay, software verification, prediction
testing and hypothesis generation. They are not sufficient on their own to recover a causal ISF
without a design that separates insulin exposure from controller response and carbohydrate
effects.

The next step is prospective validation or a carefully identified natural experiment using:

- confirmed bolus and temporary-basal delivery;
- a specified insulin-action model and sensitivity analyses across plausible models;
- explicit carbohydrate and rescue-treatment handling;
- non-overlapping evaluation windows;
- a pre-specified static comparator;
- safety outcomes as well as prediction error.

Until then, the historical fitted constants and square-root equation are research findings, not
dosing recommendations.
