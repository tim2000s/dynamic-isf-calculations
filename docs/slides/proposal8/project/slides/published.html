The published measurement

Romero-Ugalde and colleagues measured the fall in glucose per unit of insulin in 11,201 adults using Diabeloop’s DBLG1. It was 79 mg/dL per unit at a glucose of 100 mg/dL and 35 at 180. Above 300 mg/dL it was under 20.

Between 100 and 200 mg/dL the published curve falls by a factor of 2.61. The logarithmic model in AndroidAPS falls by 1.48 over the same range.

Romero-Ugalde et al., Diabetes 2025;74(Supplement 1):920-P (doi:10.2337/db25-920-P).

The proposal rests on this curve, a quartic in glucose presented at the ADA Scientific Sessions in 2025. The authors suggest halving the dose at 120 mg/dL or below and possibly doubling it above 340 mg/dL, both relative to 180. Between 100 and 200 mg/dL the published curve falls by a factor of 2.61 and the logarithmic model with a divisor of 55 falls by 1.48, so the published curve is the steeper of the two. The equation is taken as given. It was not refitted or reshaped, and this deck does not test whether it is right.
