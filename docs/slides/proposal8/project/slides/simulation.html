What happened in simulation?

40 simulated adults ran oref0 in TimSim for two months, and only the sensitivity rule differed between arms.

Against oref0 with autosens, time below 54 mg/dL was up 0.37 points (0.24 to 0.51), which reads as harm.

Time above 250 mg/dL was down 0.31 points (0.05 to 0.59), which is cleared.

Against a logarithmic rule matched at 140, below 54 was up 0.17 (0.04 to 0.32), unresolved.

Margins 0.25 and 1.25 points, a quarter of the consensus limits (Battelino 2019). Two simulated months pooled.

Now the part that didn’t go the candidate’s way. I ran 40 simulated adults on the shipped oref0 in TimSim, with a harm rule written before the runs. Over two months pooled, against oref0 with autosens, time below 54 mg/dL rose by 0.37 points (0.24 to 0.51), and the margin was 0.25, so that reads as harm on the low side. Time above 250 fell by 0.31 points (0.05 to 0.59), which clears the high side. Against a logarithmic rule matched to the same value at 140, below 54 was up 0.17 (0.04 to 0.32), which is unresolved, and above 250 was +0.09 (−0.12 to 0.29), cleared. Against the unchanged profile over both months, below 54 was up 0.35 (0.20 to 0.50) and mean glucose was 2.12 mg/dL lower (1.22 to 3.02). In the first month the rise in lows was about what a profile ISF entered 30% too low did, which was +0.39.
