Why multiply, and why one shape?

Shifting the curve up or down to meet the profile ISF gave a sensitivity of zero or below at 299 of 3,433 corrections, in 32 of 150 people. Multiplying keeps it positive.

A curve measured for each person predicted that person’s other events no better than the shared shape, in each of six cohorts.

The height comes from the profile ISF and the shape from the publication.

There are two ways to make a curve pass through somebody’s profile ISF at 140. You can add a fixed amount, which moves the whole curve up or down and keeps its shape in absolute terms, or you can multiply, which keeps its proportions. Adding looks appealing, but for people with a low profile ISF the top of the curve drops to zero or below. That happened at 299 of the 3,433 REPLACE-BG corrections, in 32 of the 150 people, and a sensitivity of zero can’t be dosed from. The other question was whether each person should have their own curve. I measured that, and a per-person shape predicted the person’s other events no better than the shared one in each of six cohorts. So the shape stays as published for everyone.
