What preceded the time below 54 mg/dL

About seven tenths of the extra time below 54 mg/dL followed a peak above 180 mg/dL. That was 0.24 points (0.14 to 0.35) after a peak of 180 to 250, and 0.06 (−0.04 to 0.18) after a peak above 250.

In the four hours before a low that followed a peak of 180 to 250, the candidate’s run had delivered a median of 11.7 U against 9.0 U on the unchanged profile. Across people that was 1.90 U more (1.02 to 2.95).

In the first simulated month, the extra time below 54 mg/dL against the unchanged profile was 0.43 points (0.24 to 0.61).

The time below 54 mg/dL was traced back to what preceded it, since a harm verdict alone does not show what to change. In the first month the candidate added 0.43 points below 54 mg/dL against the unchanged profile (0.24 to 0.61). Of that, 0.24 (0.14 to 0.35) followed a peak of 180 to 250 mg/dL and 0.06 (−0.04 to 0.18) followed a peak above 250, which together is about seven tenths. In the four hours before a low that followed a peak of 180 to 250 mg/dL, the candidate’s run had delivered a median of 11.7 U against 9.0 U on the unchanged profile. Easing near target was not the source: the first-draft arm, which only eased near target, changed time below 54 mg/dL by +0.03 points (−0.04 to 0.11). In this simulator the extra time below 54 mg/dL therefore comes from the stronger dosing on the way down from a high, which is where the simulator and the equation move in opposite directions.
