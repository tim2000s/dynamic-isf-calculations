Where did the lows come from?

About seven tenths of the extra time below 54 mg/dL followed a peak above 180: 0.24 points (0.14 to 0.35) after a peak of 180 to 250, and 0.06 (−0.04 to 0.18) after one above 250.

Before a low that followed a peak of 180 to 250, the candidate’s run had delivered a median 11.7 U against 9.0 U on the unchanged profile.

In the first simulated month, the extra time below 54 mg/dL against the unchanged profile was 0.43 points (0.24 to 0.61).

A harm reading on its own doesn’t tell you what to change, so I traced the lows back to what came before them. In the first month the candidate added 0.43 points below 54 mg/dL against the unchanged profile. Of that, 0.24 (0.14 to 0.35) followed a peak of 180 to 250 mg/dL and 0.06 (−0.04 to 0.18) followed a peak above 250, so about seven tenths of it came after a high. Before a low that followed a peak of 180 to 250, the candidate’s run had delivered a median 11.7 units against 9.0 on the unchanged profile. Easing off near target wasn’t the source, because the first-draft arm, which only eased near target, added 0.03. So in this simulator the extra lows come from the stronger dosing on the way down from a high, which is the part of the curve where the simulator and the equation disagree most.
